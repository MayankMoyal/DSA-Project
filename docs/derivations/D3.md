## Statement

At concept level, a B-tree of minimum degree t holding n keys has height at
most logₜ((n+1)/2), and because all its leaves sit at the same depth it cannot
degrade under churn. Prove the bound. No B-tree implementation is required.

## Definitions

Let t ≥ 2, n ≥ 1, and let height h count edges from the root to a leaf. Every
leaf has depth h. The root holds at least one key. Every other node holds at
least t − 1 keys, including leaves. An internal node with k keys has k + 1
children. The empty tree has no search path and is outside the logarithmic
formula.

## Minimum key count at height h

If h = 0, n ≥ 1 = 2t⁰ − 1. If h ≥ 1, the root has at least two children and
each non-root internal node has at least t children. Consequently level i has
at least 2tⁱ⁻¹ nodes for 1 ≤ i ≤ h. Including the root key and the minimum
occupancy of every other node gives

```text
n ≥ 1 + 2(t − 1)(1 + t + ⋯ + t^(h−1))
  = 1 + 2(t^h − 1)
  = 2t^h − 1.
```

It follows that t^h ≤ (n+1)/2. Since t > 1, taking the base-t logarithm
preserves the inequality, giving h ≤ logₜ((n+1)/2). For example, a degree-2
B-tree needs at least 15 keys to reach height 3 and 31 to reach height 4.

## What churn can change

A non-root split replaces one node by two nodes at its level and promotes a
separator. A merge combines two siblings at one level and moves a separator
down from their parent. When those nodes are internal, their children stay
at the next level. Redistribution changes the division of keys and child
links between siblings at the same level. A root split adds one level above
every old leaf. Removing an empty root with one child removes one level from
every leaf. These operations preserve uniform leaf depth and restore minimum
occupancy for each completed update.

At the simulator's fixed-size pair boundaries the same bound applies to every
valid B-tree shape, regardless of update history. Heights and occupancies can
vary within the bound. A search follows at most h edges, so repeated churn
cannot create the long, deep paths possible in the unbalanced BST.

## Connection to this project

The `simulate` function in `app/churn_simulator.cpp` holds n fixed at pair

variants. The B-tree result explains what a multiway occupancy invariant
