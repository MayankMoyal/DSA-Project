## Scope and published theorem

Culberson and Munro (1990) analyze exact-fit-domain updates: choose a live key uniformly, delete it, then reinsert that same value. The live key set is unchanged at pair boundaries. Their restricted long-run expected average search cost is Theta(sqrt(n)); equivalently expected internal path length is Theta(n^(3/2)). This is not a theorem for ordinary fresh-key churn. A full proof is outside this assignment. Source: https://www.ime.usp.br/~yoshi/2012i/mac323/exx/ST_ABB_Evolution/culberson_Algo.pdf

## Directional mechanism

With two children, always-successor deletion replaces a dividing key by a larger one. Returning the deleted smaller key puts it on the left side of that divider. Repeating such changes can elongate leftward paths. Example: insert 4,2,6,1,3,5,7; delete 4 and reinsert it. The root changes from 4 to 5 and its child populations from 3/3 to 4/2. This one-step imbalance example does not imply monotone growth of total depth on every update.

Figure: before deletion, 4 has children 2 and 6, with leaves 1,3,5,7. After deleting/reinserting 4, root 5 has left subtree 2(1,3(_,4)) and right subtree 6(_,7). Internal path length changes from 10 to 11.

## Our ordinary-churn model

We start with n uniformly sampled unique keys in a random insertion order from [0,16n). At each pair we delete a live key, then insert a uniformly sampled currently absent domain key. Reinsertion of the removed value is possible. Workload and randomized deletion-policy seeds are separate. This finite-domain, delete-first model is an adaptation, not an exact reproduction of Eppinger.

## Empirical source and model differences

Eppinger (1983) observed an early improvement followed by long-run deterioration with asymmetric deletion. His symmetric experiments alternated replacement sides and used insertion before deletion. Our assignment instead requests a random side choice and our simulator deletes first. We use his evidence as motivation, not a proof of a square-root law. Source: https://rtheunissen.github.io/bst/docs/references/1983_eppinger.pdf

## Targeting rule and acceleration hypothesis

shallow_exact and shallow_fresh choose the first two-child node in a breadth-first scan through depths 0..3 of a separate always-successor shape model; if none exists, they fall back to uniform deletion. The former reinserts the removed key; the latter samples a fresh absent key. Targeting shallow two-child nodes increases opportunities for successor replacements that affect large subtrees. This is a mechanistic hypothesis; biased deletion violates the theorem's uniform-selection assumption.

## Fair replay and measurement

The shape model is used only to generate inputs. Generate one ordered sequence and replay its identical blocks against all four measured strategies. Verify each pair with the sorted-array oracle, sample average depth at fixed checkpoints, and exclude setup/generation/audits from timing. Compare both final depth and time to reach 1.5 times initial depth; a schedule can win on one metric and lose on another. The independent evaluation seeds differ from the pilot seed.

## Our measured observations

At n=4000 after 16000000 pairs per run, five independent seeds gave the following always-successor depths: exact: mean final depth 28.87, sample SD 1.31; random: mean final depth 23.98, sample SD 0.73; shallow_exact: mean final depth 963.34, sample SD 45.35; shallow_fresh: mean final depth 40.76, sample SD 4.97. These are measured finite experiments using reference Structure A. The raw CSVs and report include the full trajectories, randomized-policy results and both Scapegoat comparisons. These timings describe the reference implementation used for the experiments.

## Scope of the conclusion

Call any winner the strongest tested candidate for the stated model, sizes, seeds and metrics. A biased exact-fit trace can grow much worse than the random exact-fit expectation without contradicting the theorem. Three finite sizes do not establish an asymptotic law. In ordinary churn, moving a divider upward enlarges the lower-key region available to future insertions, so a directional mechanism remains. Random insertion and deletion can also offset it; compare shallow_fresh and random directly rather than transferring the restricted theorem.

## Scapegoat adversarial target

The independent insertion generator compares ascending, descending, alternating extremes, 32-key reversed blocks and shuffled keys. Rebuild events, rebuilt nodes, rotations and time are separate objectives. The highest tested event frequency is not a proof of a globally maximizing insertion order. Galperin-Rivest: https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs/GR93.pdf. Stout-Warren: https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~qstout/pap/CACM86.pdf.
